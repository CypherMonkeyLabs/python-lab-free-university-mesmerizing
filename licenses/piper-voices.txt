Piper voices used by Python Lab
Read 2026-10-18 from each voice MODEL_CARD on Hugging Face and from the dataset page named on that card.
The repository README at https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices says `license: mit`. That label is the repo card. It is not the training-dataset licence. This file follows the per-voice card and the dataset source.

The ONNX weights are not in this repository. Only the generated MP3 waveforms are shipped.
macOS `say` was not used.

Shipped voices
All three were trained from scratch. None is a fine-tune of Lessac, Ryan, or any other restricted base.

1. en_US-ljspeech-medium (picker: Linda · United States)
Model card: https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_US/ljspeech/medium/MODEL_CARD
Card text, dataset and training sections, read 2026-10-18:

    ## Dataset

    * URL: https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/
    * License: public domain

    ## Training

    See: https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/

    US English female voice. Single speaker. Trained from scratch for 1000 epochs on medium quality settings using the LJ Speech dataset. I reencoded the recordings to a bit rate of 22500 Hz so it would match other voices released for Piper TTS.

Dataset page https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/ read 2026-10-18. Exact sentences:

    This is a public domain speech dataset consisting of 13,100 short audio clips of a single speaker reading passages from 7 non-fiction books.

    The texts were published between 1884 and 1964, and are in the public domain. The audio was recorded in 2016-17 by the LibriVox project and is also in the public domain.

    This dataset is in the public domain in the US (and most likely other countries as well). There are no restrictions on its use. For more information, please see: librivox.org/pages/public-domain.

    Recordings by Linda Johnson from LibriVox. Alignment and annotation by Keith Ito. All text, audio, and annotations are in the public domain. We request that you use this dataset for good and not evil.

Trainer page https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/ read 2026-10-18, for LJSpeech (medium): "License: public domain" and "I impose no further license or restrictions." Closing note on that page: "Feel free to use these for any legal and ethical purpose."

Commercial public use: the dataset page says there are no restrictions on its use. The model was trained from scratch, so there is no earlier restricted checkpoint in the chain.

2. en_US-norman-medium (picker: Norman · United States)
Model card: https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_US/norman/medium/MODEL_CARD
Card text, dataset and training sections, read 2026-10-18:

    ## Dataset

    * URL: https://librivox.org
    * License: public domain

    ## Training

    See: https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/

    US English male voice. Single Speaker. Trained from scratch on medium quality settings for 1200 epochs. I put together the dataset, which ended up with about 15.5 hours of recordings. All recordings came from LibriVox.org. I forgot to save the ckpt file on this one, sorry.

Dataset page https://librivox.org/pages/public-domain/ read 2026-10-18. Exact sentences:

    LibriVox records only texts that are in the public domain (in the USA – see below for why), and all our recordings are public domain (definitely in the USA, and maybe in your country as well, see below). This means anyone can use all our recordings however they wish (even to sell them).

    LibriVox recordings are in the public domain, which means people can do anything they like with them. Mostly this just means people can listen to them for free. But it also means they can: sell them (for instance on ebay), broadcast them, put them in commercials, play them at political rallies, chop them up, remix them, make music recordings of them. The recordings are free, and there is no need to credit LibriVox, although of course we much prefer if you do credit us (with a link to our site).

Trainer page https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/ read 2026-10-18, for Norman: "License: public domain", "Trained from scratch", "All recordings came from LibriVox.org." "I impose no further license or restriction" is the page's general note for these releases ("Note: Feel free to use these for any legal and ethical purpose.").

Commercial public use: LibriVox states the recordings may be sold and used in commercials. The model was trained from scratch on those recordings.

3. en_GB-cori-medium (picker: Cori · Britain)
Model card: https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_GB/cori/medium/MODEL_CARD
Card text, dataset and training sections, read 2026-10-18:

    ## Dataset

    * URL: https://librivox.org
    * License: public domain

    ## Training

    See: https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/

    UK English female voice. Single Speaker. Trained from scratch on medium quality settings for 640 epochs. I put together the dataset, which ended up with about 24 hours of recordings. All recordings came from LibriVox.org.

The LibriVox public-domain sentences quoted under Norman apply here as well. Bryce Beattie's page lists Cori (medium) as "License: public domain" and "Trained from scratch".

Commercial public use: same LibriVox terms. Trained from scratch. No restricted base.

Attribution included in the app
Public-domain terms do not require attribution. LibriVox says a credit is preferred, and LJ Speech names Linda Johnson and Keith Ito. The lab prints those credits beside the voice picker and on the Sources page.
